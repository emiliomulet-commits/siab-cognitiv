Security, permissions and compliance

Why the AI Server is the safest and most compliant way to deploy AI in a company.

07ROLE- AND PERMISSION-BASED AI

Role- and permission-based AI

The same intelligence, but each person accesses only what their role allows.

The AI Server does not give everyone the same access. Each user interacts with the intelligence according to their role: the operator gets the context of their task, the shift supervisor that of their area, management the full picture. It is not a permissions layer stuck on top that is easy to get around: the intelligence itself respects the permission before answering. If someone asks about something outside their scope, the system simply does not answer.

Why it matters. Generic AI tools do not understand hierarchies or confidentiality: they answer whoever asks. In a company, that is unworkable. Role-based control makes it possible to deploy AI across the whole organisation without opening sensitive information to people who should not see it.

08EUROPEAN PERMISSIONS PROTOCOL

European permissions protocol

Permissions follow an open, auditable European standard, not a manufacturer's proprietary rules.

The AI Server's permissions are not a closed invention of the manufacturer: they are expressed in ODRL, the standard language for describing data usage rights, in line with the UNE 0087 standard. They are machine-readable permissions: the usage rule travels attached to the data itself, not in a separate document that someone could ignore. In addition, every query and every access is logged in an auditable way.

Why it matters. Basing data governance on an open European standard provides guarantees that no proprietary rule offers: interoperability, auditability and alignment with the European regulatory framework. The customer is not tied to a manufacturer's logic.

09COMPLIANCE BY DESIGN

Compliance by design

Complying with European AI and data regulation stops being a cost and becomes a selling point.

Europe has built a demanding regulatory framework for AI and data: the AI Act, the GDPR, the Data Act, the Data Governance Act, the NIS2 cybersecurity directive and, in finance, DORA. Complying with all of that while relying on someone else's cloud is a constant battle. With the AI Server, much of compliance is structural: if data never leaves the perimeter and every access is permissioned and logged, many obligations are met by design.

Why it matters. Compliance is usually experienced as a cost and a brake. Turning it into a property of the product —and therefore into a selling point— completely changes the conversation with the customer, especially in regulated sectors.

10FULL TRACEABILITY AND AUDIT

Full traceability and audit

Every question, every answer and every action is recorded: nothing happens in the shadows.

Everything that happens on the AI Server leaves a trace. Who asked, what they asked, what the system answered, which agent acted and when: everything is recorded completely and can be consulted. AI stops being a black box and becomes an auditable system. That log is not an optional add-on: it is part of the architecture, just like permissions. What cannot be audited cannot be governed.

Why it matters. Compliance, security and risk departments need to be able to show what happened. With AI in the cloud, much of the trail is in the provider's hands. With the AI Server, the complete trail belongs to the customer and is always available for an audit, an inspection or an internal investigation.

11CYBERSECURITY THROUGH ISOLATION

Cybersecurity through isolation

What is not exposed to the Internet cannot be attacked from the Internet: a minimal attack surface.

The AI Server lives inside the customer's perimeter and does not need to be exposed to the Internet to work. That drastically reduces the attack surface: no credentials travelling to a cloud, no public endpoint to compromise, no external provider whose breach becomes your breach. Security stops depending on a chain of third parties and goes back to where the customer can control it: in-house.

Why it matters. Every connection to an external service is one more door to watch and a risk you do not fully control: many of the major data leaks of recent years have come from cloud providers. Keeping AI and data inside the perimeter removes an entire category of risk in one go.

12YOUR KNOWLEDGE DOES NOT TRAIN OTHERS

Your knowledge does not train others

Your data does not feed anyone else's model or end up benefiting your competitors.

When a company uses AI in the cloud, its questions, its documents and its know-how can end up —directly or indirectly— improving a model that anyone will later use, including its competitors. With the AI Server that does not happen: the customer's data and knowledge stay inside and work only for them. A company's knowledge is an asset. Keeping it inside prevents that asset from being diluted into a public model.

Why it matters. A company's know-how —how it produces, how it negotiates, how it solves problems— is its edge. Handing it to a shared model is giving away part of that edge. Data sovereignty protects not only privacy but also the competitive value of what the company knows.
